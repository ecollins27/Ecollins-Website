
    Debian is not merely an operating system.  Debian is a philosophy, a commitment, a way of life.  This page exists for one purpose and one purpose only: to praise Debian.  Debian slander shall NOT be tolerated.  I run Debian on my laptop, and were it not for the tragic incompatibility between its kernel and my GPU, I would run it on my PC as well.  I dare not speak of Debian Sid, for even the most devoted have their limits.

    Debian endures because Debian simply works.  It may not be the flashiest distribution, nor does it seek to be.  While Arch throws itself headfirst into the bleeding edge, and while RHEL's SELinux may eventually turn against even its most loyal servant, Debian remains steadfast.  It does not seek glory through novelty.  It seeks reliability through time, stability through discipline, and excellence through restraint.  Debian was there yesterday.  Debian is here today.  Debian will be there tomorrow.  Debian is eternal.

    Debian is efficient.  The Linux world has long wrestled with the apparent tradeoff between efficiency and convenience.  Arch achieves its leanness by leaving the user to assemble much of the machine themselves, while other operating systems pursue convenience with such abandon that they eagerly consume every byte of RAM placed before them.  Debian rejects this false dichotomy.  It is lean without being hostile, complete without being bloated, and intuitive without sacrificing control.  The very server hosting this website runs Debian and scarcely consumes 500 MB of memory.  Such efficiency is not merely a technical achievement.  It is a virtue.

    Consider, if you will, the nature of the great operating systems.  If each OS were a vehicle, Windows would be an eighteen-wheeler: enormous, heavy, unwieldy, yet prolific and indispensable when the road demands it.  Arch Linux is a drag racer: astonishingly fast, ruthlessly efficient, and always one bad adjustment away from needing its engine rebuilt.  And Debian? Debian is an old tractor.  It is not concerned with appearances.  It has no need for chrome, racing stripes, or unnecessary machinery.  It possesses precisely what is required to do the job, and it will continue doing that job long after the others have broken down and rusted.

    So let the banners be raised.  Let the kernel boot.  Let the services start.  Let the mirrors be synchronized and the apts be updated and the fastfetches run and systemd forever merciful.  In an age of needless complexity, Debian stands as a monument to simplicity.  In an age of instability, Debian stands firm.  In an age of bloat, Debian remains lean.  Through every release, every server, every late-night troubleshooting session, Debian endures.
